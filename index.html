Project 01

Industrial CAD Design & Prototyping — Cobot Interface

SolidEdgeCAD FEA3D Printing Ergonomics
The Challenge

Designing a custom mechanical interface to connect an induction brazing tool to a Fanuc cobot, to improve worker ergonomics. This was a side project alongside my main mission at Profroid, a chassis simulation (detailed in my CV).

Technical Solution
  • Full 3D modeling in SolidEdge under industrial constraints.
  • Structural reliability validation through Finite Element Analysis (FEA) and thermal-mechanical simulations.
  • Design adapted to 3D printing constraints to produce functional prototypes.
Results

A functional 3D-printed prototype of the interface, validated through simulation.

Takeaway
  • I iterated between SolidEdge models and 3D-printed parts until the prototype was fully functional.
  • Working with a senior industrial engineer helped me integrate factory safety and mechanical standards into my design process.
  • FEA and thermal simulations let me check the design against real industrial conditions before printing.

Project 02

Wind Turbine — Sustainable Design & Aerodynamic Modeling

SolidWorksSustainable Design Fluid DynamicsPhysics
The Challenge

Designing a high-performance wind turbine using recyclable materials, respecting a strict weight limit of <1 kg and specific box dimensions, able to generate current to light a LED and charge a phone.

Technical Solution
  • Architecture selection: analyzed various wind turbine types and selected a dual-stage Savonius vertical-axis design for its low-wind start-up and omnidirectional capture.
  • Optimization: applied physics laws (Betz's limit, differential drag) to optimize the "S" shape of the blades for maximum torque.
  • Parametric modeling: used SolidWorks to create a lightweight, nested structure that fits within the packaging constraints while maintaining structural integrity.
  • Materials: selected Spruce wood and flexible ABS for their balance of mechanical strength, vibration absorption, and recyclability.
Results

A fully functional prototype (base, mast, blades) capable of generating current to charge a mobile battery.

Takeaway
  • I compared several architectures to find the most efficient aerodynamic solution within strict constraints.
  • Working as a team, we aligned our technical choices with the sustainability goals to get a performant yet recyclable product.
  • This project strengthened my ability to solve geometric problems with a rigorous, systems-driven approach.

Project 03

Motion Analysis Tool – Classification of Tennis Strokes

Machine LearningIMU Sensors QuaternionsPythonData Collection
The Challenge

Develop a tool to classify tennis stroke styles to help evaluate the impact of equipment parameters on an athlete's mechanical stress and performance.

Technical Solution
  • On-court data acquisition: led experimental testing with advanced competitive players, coordinating a synchronized network of wearable sensors (IMU) to capture acceleration and rotation data.
  • Signal processing: developed a pre-processing pipeline to handle multi-segment synchronization and resolved orientation ambiguities through hemispherical quaternion alignment.
  • Machine learning: built a workflow from automated stroke segmentation to feature extraction, with a Random Forest model reaching 84% accuracy in distinguishing technical styles (topspin vs. flat).
Results

An internal tool that links athlete biomechanics to product performance, facilitating injury prevention.

Scientific status: author of the research paper "Inertial classification of topspin and flat forehand shots in tennis using wearable sensors," currently being prepared for publication.

Takeaway
  • I led this research autonomously, from high-frequency data collection to final ML validation.
  • Refining the signal processing and quaternion handling on noisy real-world data is what got the model to 84% accuracy.
  • I learned to present technical results clearly to different audiences, from athletes on the court to engineers and managers.
